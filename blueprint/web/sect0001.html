1 The Revelation Principle for communication equilibria

Players \(i \in N\) have finite type sets \(T_i\) and action sets \(A_i\). A communication device has finite message sets \(M_i\) and signal sets \(R_i\). Following Myerson (1982) and Forges (1986), we show that every communication-equilibrium outcome can be achieved by truthful-obedient play under a direct device.

Lemma 15 Truthful outcome equivalence
#

Uses

  • Definition 13

Lean declarations

  • canonical_truthful_outcome_equivalence

Truthful play under \(d^*\) has the same outcome as \(\pi \) under \(d\).

Proof ▶

The truthful profile is a point mass, so reports equal \(t\); then \(d^*\) draws from \(\mu (\cdot \mid t)\), and obeying is the identity.

Lemma 16 Deviation outcome equivalence
#

Uses

  • Definition 13
  • Definition 14

Lean declarations

  • canonical_deviation_outcome_equivalence

Deviating to \(\rho \) under \(d^*\) has the same outcome as playing the mimic of \(\rho \) under \(d\).

Proof ▶

Both sides are nested independent draws of \(x \sim \rho \) and \(y \sim \otimes _j \pi _j\); swap their order, and note that redrawing player \(i\)’s coordinate from \(\pi _i\) does not change the distribution.

Theorem 17 Revelation Principle, canonical form
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 10
  • Definition 13
  • Lemma 15
  • Lemma 16

Lean declarations

  • revelation_principle_canonical

If \(\pi \) is a communication equilibrium of \(G(d)\), then truthful play is a communication equilibrium of \(G(d^*)\) with the same outcome.

Proof ▶

A deviation’s payoff under \(d^*\) equals its mimic’s payoff under \(d\), which is at most the equilibrium payoff, which equals truthful play’s payoff.

Theorem 19 Revelation Principle
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 11
  • Definition 12
  • Theorem 18

Lean declarations

  • revelation_principle_iff

\(\mu \) is a communication-equilibrium outcome if and only if \(\mu \) is the outcome of a truthful equilibrium under some direct device.

Proof ▶

(\(\Rightarrow \)) by the existence form; (\(\Leftarrow \)) a direct device is a device.
